Ping Pong Masters

First opponent: Master Kaito

“Easy. I will play with my eyes closed.”Kaito, when you pick Easy

Sit at a full-size table and play a real match against Kaito, a retired champion from Osaka who talks the whole way through. Swing the controller like a paddle. The angle and speed of your swing decide where the ball goes.

A real rally

Bounce, spin and the net behave like the real thing. Brush up on the ball for topspin.

He has opinions

Kaito reacts to every point, streak and deuce out loud. Choose how hard he trash talks, and whether he gives you tips from your own misses.

Two rooms

Play in his lantern-lit dojo or under the lights of a tournament arena. Play from a chair and move the table until it feels right.

Meet your opponent

Moriyama Makoto was born in 1959 in Shinsekai, Osaka, and learned the game against old men in the back room of a shogi parlour, playing for coins.

The table tennis world named him Kaitō, the phantom thief, because his serves stole points. Foreign players heard “Kaito”. He stopped correcting them in the 1990s.

How to play

PaddleYour right controller is the paddle. Hold it like a handle. Left-handed players can switch hands in the menu.
ServeThe ball sits on your free hand. Pull either Trigger to toss it, then hit it.
ReturnSwing as you would with a real paddle. Brushing up gives topspin.
PauseB or Y, or the Quest menu.
MatchTo 11, win by two. Serve changes every two points, then every point from 10 all.

How it was made

Kaito is built from open MakeHuman character assets and brought to life in the browser with Meta's Immersive Web SDK and three.js. His swing, hands and reactions follow the rally as it happens. His voice is a designed character voice, not a copy of any real person, recorded ahead of time with an open speech model.

The dojo is modelled in code with its lantern light baked into the textures, so it stays smooth on the headset.

Your privacy

No account, no sign-in and no microphone. Kaito's voice is a set of recorded lines that ship with the game, so nothing you do is sent anywhere. The game keeps your settings, like table height, hand and his trash talk level, on your headset only. This site uses no cookies or analytics.
